SHUK HATIKVAH 360 - METHODS USED (what algorithm / code solves each problem)
Last updated: 13 Oct 2026 (VLM sign reading added). Keep this file current whenever a method changes.
Code: pipeline\*.py (Python 3.13, PyTorch 2.6 + CUDA 12.4, RTX 4060). Viewer: viewer\index.html.

1. INPUT / STITCHING  (pipeline\extract_frames.py, encode_video.py)
   - Camera: Insta360 ONE RS, raw dual-fisheye .insv (3968x1920, 29.97 fps).
   - Stitch: ffmpeg v360 filter, dual-fisheye -> equirectangular, lens FOV 190 deg,
     roll 180 (camera was held upside down). Fast approximate stitch; seam visible.
   - Analysis frames: 5 fps, 3840x1920 JPEG. Viewer video: 2880x1440 H.264 (NVENC).

2. VIDEO STABILISATION  (imu_extract.py, stabilize.py, bake_stab.py)
   - Gyro + accelerometer (1 kHz) read from the Insta360 trailer with telemetry-parser
     (Gyroflow's parser; runs in a separate Python 3.9 env, .venv-imu).
   - Gyro-to-camera axis mapping and time offset: fitted against the visual rotation
     between consecutive frames (ORB features on the sphere + RANSAC + Kabsch/SVD
     rotation fit), trying all 48 signed axis permutations and lags of +-0.5 s.
   - Orientation: quaternion integration of the gyro (scipy Rotation), horizon
     levelled with a complementary filter toward the accelerometer's "up".
   - Smoothing: camera heading smoothed with a Gaussian (sigma 1 s); correction
     rotation Q = R(t)^T * Ry(smoothed heading).
   - Timing fine-tuned (5 ms steps) separately for the 30 fps video and the 5 fps frames
     by minimising the remaining frame-to-frame rotation.
   - Applied OFFLINE: every pixel re-projected through Q on the GPU (torch grid_sample),
     written to video_stab\ and frames_stab\. (Render-time stabilisation was tried and
     removed - it shook.) Result: high-frequency shake about -87%, horizon level.

3. DETECTION - LEVEL 1, public classes  (detect.py)
   - YOLO11x (Ultralytics, COCO weights): person, bicycle, motorcycle, car, truck, bus,
     chair, bench, handbag, backpack, ... plus produce classes.
   - Each 360 frame is cut into 8 undistorted perspective tiles (100 deg FOV, pitch -15,
     1024 px; precomputed cv2.remap grids); boxes mapped back to yaw/pitch on the sphere.
   - Raw detections saved at confidence >= 0.05 (cached once); the display threshold is
     applied later (viewer default 0.6).

4. DETECTION - LEVEL 2, open vocabulary  (detect.py --prompts level2)
   - YOLO-World v2 (yolov8x-worldv2, CLIP ViT-B/32 text encoder) with 28 text prompts:
     crates, cardboard, plastic bag, organic waste, trash bin, hand truck, shopping
     trolley, walker, mobility scooter, e-bike, broom, display table, flag, lamp, ...
   - Same tiling and caching as level 1.

5. DUPLICATES ACROSS TILES  (tracks.py: dedup_frame)
   - Non-maximum suppression in yaw/pitch space (wrap-aware angular IoU > 0.35),
     preferring the tile where the box is least truncated (centrality score).

6. TRACKING (persistent IDs)  (tracks.py: track, stitch)
   - Frame-to-frame assignment with the Hungarian algorithm (lap.lapjv).
   - Similarity = max(angular IoU, Gaussian of centre distance scaled by box height),
     with a size-ratio gate; constant-velocity yaw prediction; tracks kept 2 s unseen.
   - Tracklet stitching: a track ending and another starting within 3 s near the
     extrapolated position with a similar size are merged.
   - Objects (level 1 and 2) tracked the same way, per class.

7. BOX SMOOTHING  (tracks.py: smooth_tracks; viewer)
   - Gaps up to 1 s filled by linear interpolation; centre smoothed with a zero-phase
     Gaussian (sigma 0.3 s), box size with sigma 0.6 s (offline, no lag).
   - Viewer: boxes interpolated per track ID between 5 fps frames (smooth at 30 fps),
     fade in/out, tracks shorter than 1 s hidden, non-overlapping labels (closest first).

8. CAMERA OPERATOR REMOVAL  (tracks.py: operator_like; stabilize.py: operator_mask_yaw)
   - Fingers: boxes entirely below -35 deg pitch dropped. Body: very large boxes.
   - Operator's camera-fixed direction = most frequent yaw of "huge, very close" person
     boxes; detections inside +-45 deg of it and very close are dropped (on stabilised
     frames, boxes are rotated back to camera directions with Q first).

9. DISTANCE AND CAMERA HEIGHT  (tracks.py)
   - Camera height self-calibrated: median over many people of the height implied by
     their box (assumed person height 1.70 m). Result 1.37 m / 1.20 m (stabilised).
   - Ground distance = camera height / tan(angle of the feet below the horizon).

10. PERCEIVED GENDER + AGE GROUP  (attributes.py)
   - CLIP ViT-L/14 zero-shot on full-body crops (faces too small in 360 footage):
     up to 5 largest, confident crops per track; undistorted crop with neighbours
     greyed out; prompt ensembles ("a photo of a man/woman ...", age groups).
   - Probabilities averaged per track; label only if >= 0.75, else "uncertain";
     tracks with no crop >= 6 deg tall are "uncertain".
   - Age groups follow the team's coding scheme (18-35 / 36-60 / 61+, child).
   - Crops are not stored; results shown only in aggregate. Not yet validated.

11. OCR OF SIGNS  (ocr_signs.py; pipeline\ocr\ copied from C:\Projects\OCR)
   - Text detection: docTR db_resnet50 on 16 tiles (8 at eye level, 8 tilted up for shop
     signs), every 2nd analysis frame; word boxes merged into text lines.
   - Lines grouped into signs across frames with the tracker from section 6.
   - Recognition: Tesseract 5.5 (heb+eng, page segmentation mode 7 = single line) on the
     2 best crops per sign, each upscaled + CLAHE + unsharp mask, normal and inverted
     (light text on dark boards); best mean word confidence wins.
   - Correction: HebrewFinalFixer (final letters, samekh/final-mem swaps) + SymSpell
     spellcheck (Hebrew 100k frequency list + English dictionary). Signs below confidence
     55 are marked "unreadable", not guessed.
   - RESULT (13 Oct): weak. ~2,000 signs per walk detected, ~1,000 large enough to try,
     only 6-11% reach confidence 55; manual spot check of the best 24: ~3 fully correct,
     ~6 partly, ~15 wrong. Detection (docTR) works; Tesseract recognition on painted/angled
     Hebrew does not. Also found: sign grouping too loose (neighbouring lines merged) and the
     operator's T-shirt read as a sign.
   - CURRENT READER (13 Oct, ocr_vlm.py): text lines grouped into SIGNS across frames (seen >= 2
     times, largest line >= 18 px); one crop per sign at its best frame containing all its lines;
     crops laid out on numbered contact sheets (16 per sheet); each sheet read by Claude (vision)
     running as background agents inside the Claude Code session (no API key), output per crop:
     text, language, legible yes/no, kind (shop sign / price sign / banner / packaging / other /
     no text / operator's shirt). Merged into ocr_vlm_stab\signs.parquet; the viewer uses it.
   - Sign boxes are tracked and smoothed like people/objects: one box per sign per frame,
     gaps <= 1 s filled (covers the every-2nd-frame text detection), zero-phase smoothing; the
     viewer interpolates, fades and applies the 1 s minimum-length filter to signs too.
   - RESULT: walk 1 1,151 of 1,934 sign crops read, walk 2 1,093 of 1,817 (Tesseract: 64 / 113).
     Mostly Hebrew, plus English, Russian, Arabic and mixed. 683 crops were the operator's T-shirt and
     331 had no text - both removed. Independent spot check of 16 random readings: all correct or
     correct visible fragments. Known inconsistency: readers differed on whether number-only crops
     (prices, phones) count as legible.

12. CACHING ("compute once")  (common.py: Stage)
   - Every expensive stage writes outputs + manifest.json (stage version, parameters,
     input fingerprints) and is skipped when unchanged. Cheap stages always rerun.

13. VIEWER  (viewer\index.html, pipeline\serve.py, start_viewer.bat)
   - WebGL fragment shader renders the equirectangular video as a perspective view;
     overlays projected with the same yaw/pitch maths (pipeline\spherical.py).
   - Local server with HTTP Range + HTTP/1.1 (needed for video seeking on Windows).
   - Boxes carry an EMOJI badge per class (e.g. person, trolley, crate, bin, sign) instead of text;
     track ID, confidence, distance, time seen and sign text are in the card that opens on click.
   - People boxes are coloured by estimated age group on ONE green ramp (light = child/young,
     dark = older adult; grey = uncertain) with a matching emoji; the panel's age graph uses the
     same colours. Perceived gender is shown per person as the emoji badge background
     (blue = man, pink = woman, grey = uncertain; user decision 13 Oct). Both estimates are
     unvalidated; visible errors exist (e.g. a man seen from behind labelled woman).
   - Counts are shown as "in view / whole walk" (e.g. 13 / 565). Whole-walk totals count UNIQUE
     TRACK IDs that pass the same filters as the view: the track's highest confidence >= the slider
     (level 2: max(0.15, slider - 0.2)) and, with "hide tracks < 1 s", a length >= 1 s. Because
     tracks still fragment (one person can get 2+ IDs), totals are upper bounds until validated.

14. GIS EXPORT AND VALIDATION  (export_overlays.py, make_validation.py, viewer\validate.html)
   - GeoJSON of persistent entities (track ID, frames, time, confidence); route position
     from an assumed walking pace until anchor points arrive (no GPS in the videos).
   - Hand-count page: 30 frames, your count vs the model's count (target +-15%).

15. MAP: WALKS ON THE GIS MAP WITHOUT GPS  (georef.py; config\anchors.json; viewer minimap)
   - The videos have no GPS. The team gave anchors: at frame F the stabilised view at yaw Y looks at a
     known store (name or GlobalID in Shops_In_hatikva_30_7_26.xlsx, WGS84 X/Y).
   - Heading per frame = integrated, smoothed 1 kHz gyro heading (stabilize.py -> heading.json).
   - Path = dead reckoning: p(f) = P0 + sum v*dt*[sin th, cos th], th = alpha + heading + beta
     (camera carried at a fixed angle to the walking direction), piecewise-constant speed between anchors.
   - Unknowns P0, alpha, beta, speeds fitted by robust least squares (scipy, soft-L1): every anchor's
     view bearing must point at its store; soft priors: store 1.5-15 m from the camera, ~0.9 m/s pace,
     smooth pace changes; multi-start over alpha/beta.
   - Stage 1 alone fitted the anchors to ~2 deg but put 50% (walk 1) / 33% (walk 2) of the path inside
     buildings and ran through stalls. Stage 2 = map matching on OpenStreetMap (api.openstreetmap.org,
     cached as data/raw/osm/hatikva_osm.xml; the store points lie a median 3.3 m from OSM street
     centrelines, so OSM and the team's GIS agree):
       * gyro-drift and pace corrections, linear between knots every 20 s (drift prior 12 deg, smooth);
       * distance fields on a 0.5 m grid (scipy distance_transform_edt, bilinear lookup):
         depth inside an OSM building (penalised hard), distance to a walkable street centreline
         (free band 3 m), distance to any store point (stalls are obstacles: keep >= 2 m);
       * anchors as sight-ray miss distance in metres (sideways miss + "store behind the camera"),
         noise = 1.5 m stall/GIS position + 8 deg pointing. Angles are misleading for stores 2-5 m away
         straight ahead/behind (most of the team's anchors), where 2 m sideways = 40+ deg.
   - Result (stage 2): inside buildings walk 1 0.4% / walk 2 2.8%; median distance to a street
     centreline 1.6 / 1.4 m; within 1 m of a store point 1.4% / 2.7%; anchor sight-ray miss median
     2.6 / 3.3 m; paths 272 / 231 m. Consistent outliers in every variant: Melech Haavatiach (walk 1,
     ~10 m) and the GlobalID stall (walk 2, ~10 m): check those anchors. 'Pinat Habriut' is not in the
     store table.
   - Stage 3, centring between the store rows (user feedback: the walker must walk between the stalls,
     with stores on both sides): at each frame take the store points within +-8 m along the path; the
     widest lateral gap >= 3 m nearest the walker is the walkway -> move to its middle; with one row only,
     keep 3 m off it on the side nearer an OSM street centreline. Shifts smoothed (Gaussian, sigma 3 s),
     backed off where they would enter a building. Median shift 1.5 m (walk 1) / 0.9 m (walk 2).
     Stores within 10 m of walk 2 now split 13 left / 13 right; walk 1's south stretch has stores in the
     table on one side only, so there the path keeps 3 m off that row.
   - IMU step energy (1 s std of |accel|, stabilize.py -> heading.json): the walker never stops in either
     walk, so a smooth pace model is adequate.
   - Store -> video: closest approach of each walk to each store (<= 22 m); view yaw = store bearing
     minus the frame's map bearing of yaw 0 (drift-corrected). 138 of 220 stores are clickable.
   - Viewer: Leaflet 1.9.4 + OpenStreetMap tiles. Heading-up round minimap (GTA-style) with route,
     store dots and view cone; click -> full map with all stores coloured by trade and a live
     "you are here" marker + view cone; store popup
     "Go to video" switches walk if needed and seeks/turns to the store. The full map always opens
     centred on the current position (zoom 19).

16. PEOPLE ON THE MAP + MOTION ("OPTICAL FLOW" OF PEOPLE)  (person_flow.py, motion.py; viewer "Motion & paths")
   - Position: camera position (georef.py) + ground distance along the map bearing of the person's feet
     (bearing = yaw0_bearing + yaw_foot; distance = camera height / tan(foot depression), flat ground),
     1-12 m from the camera only.
   - v0.7 was wrong (user: "the directions are not true"). Diagnosis with STATIC ground objects (parked
     motorcycles, chairs, crates, tables, bins, stools) run through the same pipeline, which must read 0 m/s:
       * the map-fit camera speed was up to ~2x too fast and ~15 deg off locally: subtracting it made people
         walking towards the camera look standing and static things look walking (static median 0.75 m/s);
       * Gaussian smoothing mirrors the data at track ends: short tracks came out at ~0 m/s;
       * tracker ID switches (e.g. onto the operator) jumped positions.
   - Fixes:
       * camera ego-motion measured in the video: each static object implies camera velocity =
         -(its velocity relative to the camera); per frame the geometric median over static objects within
         +-2.4 s (map-fit velocity where there are too few). Same distance scale as the people.
       * local linear fit (Gaussian weights, sigma 1 s position / 1.5 s velocity) instead of smoothing;
       * tracks cut at impossible jumps (> 1 m + 3 m/s), longest piece kept;
       * second, independent estimate from dense optical flow (person_flow.py, cached): DIS flow between
         consecutive stabilised frames; feet motion (tracked box) minus the median flow of the ground right
         beside the feet (same distance, so it moves like a static point there: camera translation, rotation
         residuals and horizon tilt cancel); converted to m/s with the distance at the feet; 2 s rolling median.
       * velocity = average of both. Arrow only when 0.35-2.5 m/s and both estimates point the same way
         (within 60 deg); otherwise a ring (standing) or a dashed ring with "?" (moving, direction uncertain).
   - Validation:
       * static objects held out from the ego-motion estimate: median apparent speed 0.75 m/s (v0.7) ->
         0.33 geometry / 0.42 flow / 0.29 averaged; 25% would still be classed walking, hence the agreement
         gate for arrows.
       * the two estimates agree within 45 deg for 80% of clearly moving people (peak at 0 deg: no sign error);
         radial components correlate 0.71, lateral 0.36.
       * visual check walk 1 t=48-49 s: a shopper approaching the camera moves ~0.3-0.5 m in 0.8 s relative to
         a crate and a paper on the ground (0.4-0.6 m/s); pipeline 0.4 m/s. Standing man -> ring; man with a
         walker -> arrow towards the camera.
       * reliable direction available for 58% (walk 1) / 69% (walk 2) of moving frames.
   - Re-linking on the map: track B continues track A if B starts <= 2 s after A ends, within 1 m + 0.4 m/s
     x gap of the predicted position (forward and backward), unambiguous, and perceived gender does not
     conflict. ~29 / 8 re-links.
   - Viewer: the card opens by clicking the emoji badge (bigger, easier target; boxes are clickable only
     when emoji badges are switched off).
   - Viewer: ground arrow at the feet (projected from the map back into the 360 view), mode None / Followed /
     All in view / Walking / Standing; follow by card button, Shift + drag box, or everyone in view; one
     colour per followed person on box, arrow, chip and map paths.
   - Limits: flat ground; position error ~0.5 m at 3 m, ~2 m at 10 m (+ route error, shared by everyone);
     noise floor ~0.3 m/s; individual directions not hand-validated beyond the spot checks above.

17. PEOPLE ON SIGNS -> CATEGORY possible_rabi_sign  (depicted.py; config\depicted_review.json)
   - Problem: the person detector (YOLO11x) also fires on pictured people: rabbi portraits, posters, adverts,
     framed photos, menu boards. They must not be counted as people.
   - Proposal (automatic): CLIP ViT-L/14 zero-shot on the best crops WITH context (sign frame visible):
     "real person in a market" vs "printed picture / portrait / poster of a person" -> p_depicted; plus
     implied body height on flat ground, feet above the horizon, closeness to detected text regions.
     Candidates = p_depicted >= 0.3 (17 tracks walk 1, 36 walk 2).
   - Decision (vision review, like the sign reading): contact sheets of the candidates checked by Claude ->
     depicted / operator / real, stored in config\depicted_review.json. Why: automatic rules alone were not
     reliable (CLIP >= 0.75 also fired on people behind glass, drivers and the operator's printed T-shirt;
     the size rule fired on far real people). Unreviewed tracks fall back to p_depicted >= 0.9 (~75%
     precision on the reviewed set).
   - Recall check: 238 further tracks that sit on signs or have an implausible size were reviewed; no other
     pictures found.
   - Result: walk 1: 7 possible_rabi_sign + 2 operator tracks; walk 2: 15 + 5. Removed from people counts,
     gender/age statistics, motion and map paths; shown as their own layer (yellow dotted box, picture emoji)
     and exported to GeoJSON as class possible_rabi_sign.
   - Side finding: ~35 more walk-1 person tracks are the camera operator (arm, T-shirt) that the operator
     filter in tracks.py misses -> to fix next.

18. FOOD ON DISPLAY  (detect.py --model yolov8x-oiv7.pt; food.py)
   - WHERE: YOLOv8x trained on Open Images V7 (600 classes, Ultralytics release weights) on the stabilised
     frames, food classes (conf >= 0.15), plus COCO produce already cached from YOLO11x (banana, apple, orange,
     carrot, broccoli; conf >= 0.3). On market displays OIV7 names ~98% of its food boxes only generically
     ("Vegetable", "Food", "Fruit"), so it is used for location only.
   - Tracking: all food boxes tracked as one class (no label flicker) with the level-2 tracker; a track is
     a display/stack, not single items.
   - WHAT: CLIP ViT-L/14 zero-shot over a market vocabulary of 41 items (tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers,
     eggplants, herbs, watermelons, bananas, citrus, dates, nuts, spices, olives, bread/pita, pastries,
     cheese, meat, fish, eggs, prepared food, mixed fruit & vegetables, ...), averaged over the 3 widest crops.
   - Printed food (menu boards, fruit drawings on signs): own category "Food picture (menu/sign)", shown
     with a picture emoji and NOT counted as food on display (walk 2 is full of restaurant menu photos).
   - Thresholds from sample checks (contact sheets): named when p >= 0.5 (~80% correct; below 0.5 ~20%,
     so shown as "Produce (unsure)"); food picture when p >= 0.8 (7/8 correct; 0.6-0.8 were mostly real
     stalls); "Prepared food" needs p >= 0.7 (it attracted colourful produce), else the runner-up label.
   - Result: walk 1: 1,009 displays (504 unsure, 232 bananas, 107 mixed, 35 food pictures, potatoes,
     tomatoes, watermelons, meat...); walk 2 (Hamevasser St): 183 (83 menu/sign pictures, 28 meat).
   - Limits: no hand-labelled ground truth yet (planned: ~30 stall crops per walk); bananas are
     over-represented because COCO detects them very often (one stand -> several tracks).

19. WASTE ON THE FLOOR  (detect.py --prompts waste; waste.py)
   - Candidates: YOLO-World with a waste prompt set (litter, crumpled paper, paper cup, plastic bottle, drink
     can, plastic wrapper, plastic bag / flattened cardboard on the ground, torn cardboard box, garbage bag,
     vegetable leaves / crushed fruit on the ground, food waste, napkin) plus look-alike prompts that are
     dropped (shoe, manhole cover, drain grate, crate of produce, puddle); plus waste-like level-2 classes.
     conf >= 0.1, below the horizon, small boxes.
   - On the walkway only: SegFormer-B2 (ADE20K, nvidia/segformer-b2-finetuned-ade-512-512) segments the lower
     band of every stabilised frame (pitch +5..-75 deg); floor = floor, road, sidewalk, earth, path, runway,
     dirt track; masks cached per frame (reusable for walkway width / encroachment). A candidate is kept when
     the ring around it is >= 50% floor AND the strip just under it is >= 60% floor (not a table or stall),
     and it overlaps no person box by more than 30% (carried bags).
   - Tracked as one class (level-2 tracker), then CLIP ViT-L/14 on up to 3 crops: 6 waste types (cardboard,
     paper & litter, plastic bag, bottle / can, organic waste, garbage bag) vs not-waste (stock boxes, clean
     floor, feet, drains, puddles, trolleys, carried bags, bins, tables, vehicles); waste when the waste
     prompts sum to >= 0.5.
   - Items at the same map spot (<= 1 m, <= 10 s apart) are merged (tracker splits); short gaps (<= 1 s)
     inside an item are interpolated. Map position = camera position + ground distance along the bearing;
     route position = camera chainage; density = items per 10 m.
   - Result: walk 1: 19 items (12 cardboard, 2 plastic bags, 2 bottles/cans, 2 organic, 1 garbage bag),
     max 3 per 10 m; walk 2: 11 items (7 cardboard, 2 paper & litter, 1 bottle/can, 1 organic).
   - STATUS: results saved (waste_stab.json, waste.parquet) but NOT shown in the viewer (user decision 13 Oct:
     not good enough yet). Viewer flag SHOW_WASTE = false.
   - Visual checks (contact sheets): first version 35-40% correct (carried bags, bins, cafe tables); after
     the person-overlap, floor-under and look-alike filters ~67% (walk 1, 14/21 before merging) and ~73%
     (walk 2, 8/11). Remaining errors: goods for sale on the floor (detergent bottles, crates), a bag hanging
     at a stall. Recall not measured: small litter (cigarette butts, wrappers) is below the detector's
     resolution at 5 fps / 1024 px tiles.

20. STALL MATERIALS (what the stalls are made of)  (stall_materials.py; config\stall_review.json)  - not shown yet
   - Crops: per GIS store, the best side views (store 2-8 m away, 40-140 deg off the walking direction, so the
     frontage is visible and the operator is behind) within +-8 s of the closest approach; 3 undistorted crops,
     width scaled to the distance. v1 (closest-approach frame only) often showed the street or the operator.
   - CLIP ViT-L/14 zero-shot on the survey questions (facade Open/Glass/Metal; structure steel/blocks/cement)
     and 8 detailed materials. vs survey: facade 82.7%, structure 83.5% (99 stores) - BELOW the majority
     baseline (always Open / steel: 93% / 97% among visible stores); detailed materials almost never fire.
   - Vision review (Claude, 5 contact sheets): facade, structure and visible materials for 99 stores; 91 show
     the store. Materials seen: steel frame 50, glass 28, metal roller shutter 17, fabric awning 9, plastic
     crates 8, ceramic tiles 7, wood 7, cardboard 6, corrugated metal 2, plastic sheeting 2.
     Agreement with the survey: facade 78%, structure 59%.
   - Why the disagreement (checked store by store): (1) store association - stores are 2-3 m wide and densely
     packed while the route is ~2-3 m accurate, so several stores' crops show the same dominant frontage (5
     stores -> the glass "BAR" front, 6 -> the Pizuchei Shadi glass shop); (2) definitions - block-built butcher
     shops with glass counters and steel shutters are coded "Open / steel" in the survey, so the survey's
     Skeleton_Mat / Facade_Mat seem to mean something else (or are defaults).
   - Next: associate crops to stores by shop names read on signs (OCR already reads e.g. "פיצוחי שאדי"); ask
     Boaz for the survey definitions; or report materials per frontage segment along the route instead of
     per GIS store.
   - SHOWN AS LOCATIONS (user decision 13 Oct: location-based, not store-based; frontage.py): each reviewed
     crop is placed where the camera looked (camera position at the view frame + distance along the view
     bearing), with route chainage and side; crops of the same frontage within 3 m are merged -> 82 frontage
     points (walk 1: 66, walk 2: 16). Viewer: 🧱 markers on both maps (popup: crop, facade, structure,
     materials, "Go to video"), a clickable 🧱 badge in the 360 view while passing (+-3 s), layer
     "Frontage materials (experimental)". Not tied to GIS store names.

21. VIEWER DESIGN VARIANTS  (viewer/engine.js; viewer/v1_atelier ... v5_mapfirst.html; start_viewer_1..5_*.bat)
   - Shared engine: WebGL equirect rendering (same maths as before), data loading, per-frame entity list
     blended between 5 fps analysis frames with the same confidence / 1 s filters, counts and walk totals by
     unique track ID, camera position/heading on the map, ground motion arrows, drag/zoom/keys, hit testing.
   - Design rules (UX critique 13 Oct): fixed zones that never reflow; settings separated from information;
     a visual budget for overlays (quiet dots, focus lens, nearest-N, one mode at a time); one colour-blind-safe
     palette (Okabe-Ito) with one hue per category; numbers and icons before text; light themes.
   - The five pages were written in parallel by sub-agents from one brief each, with v1 as the reference;
     each was checked in the browser (HTTP 200, console free of errors, busy scene at walk 1 0:49).

22. SEGMENT SUMMARY BETWEEN TWO STORES  (viewer/index.html, full map: "Select segment")
   - The user picks a start and an end store on the full map. Each store is projected onto every walk
     (closest route point); the walk with the smallest worst-case distance (<= 30 m) is used, and the
     segment = the analysis frames between the two closest points.
   - Summary (same filters as the viewer: min confidence, hide tracks < 1 s): length (route metres),
     video time; people = unique track IDs in the frames, per 100 m, most at once, age group, perceived
     gender, movement (motion.py state); stores whose closest approach lies in the segment (<= 22 m),
     by trade; food displays by item (menu/sign pictures counted apart); signs read (text); objects and
     market items (unique tracks by class); pictured people on signs; frontage-material points in the
     segment. Waste excluded (hidden layer).
   - Export: JSON (full structure) and Excel (SheetJS xlsx 0.18.5, loaded on demand) with sheets
     Summary, Stores, People, Food, Signs, Objects, Frontage. Checked: walk 1 Melech Haavatiach -> next
     stall south, 97 m, 223 people, 41 stores, counts cross-checked against the overlay JSON in Python.

23. CROWDS  (crowds.py; viewer full map + minimap)
   - Heuristic (user idea): people whose boxes touch or overlap and have similar size (box height ratio
     >= 0.7, so similar distance) are linked; a crowd = connected group of >= 3 people in a frame; crowds
     are followed over time (share >= 1 person, best Jaccard, gaps <= 1 s), kept if they last >= 1 s.
   - Inputs filtered first: viewer defaults (track max conf >= 0.6, >= 1 s); pictured people / operator from
     depicted.py; NEW operator filter (median box height >= 58 deg anywhere, or >= 48 deg within +-60 deg of
